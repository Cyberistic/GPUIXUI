# GPUix capabilities — what the pinned runtime provides, what it does not

`MISSION.md` forbids presenting a capability the runtime lacks as if it worked.
This document is the durable record: one row per capability, the verdict, the
evidence, and the strongest honest alternative. Component plans and
`COMPONENTS.md`'s **GPUix support** column cite this file instead of restating a
verdict, and the documentation site publishes the reader-facing subset at
`/docs/runtime`.

**Pinned runtime:** `@gpuix/react` / `@gpuix/native` **0.11.0**, Apache-2.0. The
rows were re-read against the installed typings on 2026-10-21 — `StyleDesc`,
`HostProps`, `ElementType` and the host props in
`node_modules/@gpuix/native/dist/host.d.ts`, and the renderer methods in
`index.d.ts`. Where a row says *measured*, a probe in `apps/playground` reports
the fact from a real paint, not from a type; the probe id is in the row and its
measurement is in `apps/playground/README.md`.

**Two levels of evidence, kept apart.** A typed field proves an API is
*publishable*; only a probe proves it *paints*. A row marked `unverified` has
neither and must not be assumed to work.

## 1. Host elements

`ElementType` is exactly:

```ts
"div" | "text" | "img" | "svg" | "canvas" | "input" | "textarea" | "anchored" | "code" | "diff" | "markdown" | "virtual-list";
```

`canvas` is a blank host element — it appears in the union but has no
2D-context or draw props, so custom drawing is **not** available. `anchored`
positions its content and paints no surface of its own (the overlay packages put
the surface on a child `div`). `virtual-list` takes either children — retained in
the React tree, with only the off-screen build and paint skipped — or
`itemCount` + `estimatedItemHeight` + `windowStart` plus a row renderer, where the
app mounts the slice; a compound-children list therefore cannot be truly windowed
through it.

## 2. Provided by the runtime

Headless React components in `@gpuix/react` 0.11.0: `Select` + parts, `Combobox`
+ parts, `Tooltip` + parts, `Button`/`buttonProps` (`@gpuix/react/button`),
`Dialog` + parts, `AnimatePresence`/`motion`, and `floating`'s `FloatingLayer`,
`renderSlot`, `useControllableState`, `mergeStyles` and `StateStyle`. These give
behaviour and positioning; they ship no visual design.

Renderer methods the library builds on (`@gpuix/native` `index.d.ts`):
`focusElement`, `focusNext`, `focusPrevious`, `focusNextWithin`,
`focusPreviousWithin`, `getFocusedElementId`, `getElementBounds`,
`getElementsAt`, `getAllText`, `getPaintedText`, `getPaintedHighlights`,
`getScrollOffset`, `getSelectedText`, `promptForPaths`, `registerFont`,
`setImage`, `setImagePixels`, and the `simulate*`/automation surface the tests
drive.

## 3. The limits

Verdicts: **supported** · **supported, with limits** · **not supported** ·
**unverified** (absent from the typings and not measured — never assume it).

| Capability | Verdict | Evidence | Workaround |
| ---------- | ------- | -------- | ---------- |
| Flexbox and grid | supported | `StyleDesc.display`, `flexDirection`, `flexWrap`, `gap`/`rowGap`/`columnGap`, `alignItems`, `justifyContent`; `gridTemplateColumns`/`Rows` are integers | — |
| Grid span | not supported | no `gridColumn`/`gridRow`; only `gridColumnMin`/`gridRowMin` track sizing | size the track, or give the item an explicit width |
| Two-stop linear gradients | supported, with limits | `LinearGradientBackground.stops` is a 2-tuple; `angle` in degrees; `colorSpace` `srgb` or `oklab`. **Measured** by `box-paint` on a 240×60 box (`#FF3B30` → `#0A84FF` at 90°): the 10% sample paints `231,67,69`, the 90% sample `34,124,234`, and neither is the page `17,20,26`, so the fill grades rather than falling back to one stop | a gradient is real but has exactly **two** stops: stack divs or use `svg` for a three-stop scale |
| Multi-stop, radial, conic, repeating gradients | not supported | the stop tuple holds exactly two | paint a stack of divs, or an `svg` |
| `boxShadow`, including `inset` | supported, with limits | `BoxShadow` is a structured object with `inset`, and a window was measured by `elevation-paint` (27 checks): a shadow with `offsetY: 16` and no blur paints the **118×14** band under its box in its own colour — **6 608** of 6 608 pixels at the capture's 2× scale — leaves the box's own fill alone, and paints nothing **4** points above the box or **48** below it. A blur still paints past the edge and fades with distance (`115,36,35` at 4 points out, `74,29,31` at 16, the page at 48); a spread paints every side; an `inset` shadow paints the same ring on the **inside** (the shadow's colour 4 points inside the top edge, the fill at the centre, the page 4 points outside) while the same geometry without `inset` paints outside and leaves the fill inside; a **list** paints every entry (red below, blue above); and a colour the parser cannot read paints **no** shadow, which is the silent flat surface `invalidShadowColors` reports. A **level** is real but soft: the dark theme's `lg` on a 220×120 surface composites **`12,14,18`** where the page is `17,20,26` — a 5/6/8-byte darkening 20 points below the box, because a 56-point blur spreads the level's 0.8 alpha across the edge — while `sm` paints the page exactly at the same point | a level is a soft edge, not a cast shadow: do not expect one to carry separation on its own — pair a raised surface with a border or a role change when it must be found at a glance. An unparseable shadow colour fails silently, so keep `auditTheme`/`invalidShadowColors` in the app's gate. `inset` is the only way to shade a well or a recessed band without a second element |
| Per-corner radii, per-side border widths, a two-value `borderStyle` | supported | `StyleDesc` corner and side fields; `borderStyle` is `solid` or `dashed`. **Measured** by `box-paint`: a 2-point solid border round a 160×60 box paints **3 456** border pixels and the same geometry dashed paints **2 240** — 65% of the edge, in **27** dashes of 8 device pixels (4 points) with a 2-point gap; `borderTopLeftRadius: 24` cuts the top-left corner to the page while the top-right keeps its fill; `borderTopWidth: 8` paints 8 points of top edge with no left border. `@gpuixui/tw` compiles no dashed, per-corner or per-side utility, so the per-corner radii and the per-side widths pass through `style`. A **dashed** edge does not have to: the primitives' `Box` takes `borderStyle="dashed"` — and `paddingX`/`paddingY` for the two axes — as typed props, and its own `box.mdx` documents them | a dashed edge costs a third of a solid one, so do not measure a border by counting pixels; a table rule or a tab underline needs one side, not a full border |
| Opacity | supported, with limits | `StyleDesc.opacity`, and a window was measured by `opacity-paint` (16 checks): `1` paints the fill exactly, `0` paints the page exactly, `0.5` paints **`136,39,37`** — the `255,59,48` fill's channel and the `17,20,26` page's added at the share (`136`,`39.5`,`37`) — and `0.25` paints **`76,30,31`**, nearer the page on every channel. A share **1.5** paints the fill, so a value above one clamps rather than brightening. The share reaches the **subtree**: a full-colour line inside a half-opacity parent paints **`0`** pixels of its exact ink where the same line inside an opaque parent paints **`1091`**, and an opaque `#0A84FF` shadow under a half-opacity box paints **`13,76,140`** — the shadow mixed with the page at the same share | one share fades a whole subtree, ink and shadow included, so a control that needs one part to stay legible fades per element instead of setting `opacity` on its root. A faded surface's shadow fades too, so a ghost cannot look raised; the mix is in byte space, so a composited token pair can be predicted from `rgba` arithmetic |
| `aspectRatio` | supported, with limits | `StyleDesc.aspectRatio` — width divided by height, applied to the axis that is not definite — and the library's `Box` hands it through. **Measured** by `aspect-ratio-paint` (14 checks on 0.11.0), because a window reports no style and only a painted box says which dimension the ratio fills: a definite width of **160** at ratio **2** paints **160×80**, a **100**-wide box at ratio **0.5** paints **100×200** (a portrait box — a ratio below one makes the box taller, not smaller), a definite height of **60** at ratio **2** paints **120×60**, two definite dimensions (**160×60**) keep both rather than letting the ratio resize one, a child stretched by a **240**-wide column paints **240×120**, and a child stretched by an **80**-tall row paints **0×80** — the row supplies the height, but the ratio derives **no width** from it | let the ratio own the axis left open: reserve one dimension and the ratio fills the other. A child inside a **row** must declare its height and let the ratio derive the width (the `120×60` case), because the row's stretch makes only the height definite and a zero width paints nothing; two definite dimensions leave the ratio unconsulted, so a component that changes its ratio at runtime must also leave one axis open |
| Text truncation | supported, with limits | `whiteSpace: "nowrap"`, `textOverflow` (`ellipsis`, `ellipsis-start`) and `lineClamp` are in `StyleDesc`; a **window** was measured by `text-truncation` (30 checks), because the declarations alone do not say what paints. A 34-character run in a 200-point cell clipped with `overflow: "hidden"` keeps one line and the ink stops at the cell's edge — the last glyph is **sliced**, not marked. `textOverflow: "ellipsis"` keeps one line and ends the ink **186** points in (14 short of the edge) with a mark at the tail: the last 12 points carry **7** upper pixels where a stem carries **82**. `ellipsis-start` marks the **head** (**14**) and keeps a whole glyph at the tail (ink to **197.5**). A `<text>` that carries `width` + `overflow` + `textOverflow` itself truncates the same way, so no wrapper is needed. **`lineClamp` paints nothing**: a paragraph that wraps to five lines still paints five with `lineClamp: 2` on the text and with it on the box, and the box stays 108 points tall; a definite two-line height with `overflow: "hidden"` painted two lines (48-point box). The shipped `Text`'s `clamp` prop was measured in the same window painting five lines too, and that was the component's defect rather than the route: `clamp` now resolves the definite box itself — the role's leading times the line count, with a hidden overflow — and no longer sets `lineClamp`, so the package's runtime suite holds a one-line and a two-line clamp at exactly one and two leadings (`layout.runtime.test.tsx`). The probe now reads the fixed component: the shipped `Text`'s `clamp` prop paints **two** lines of the paragraph and its box is only as tall as those two lines (48 points), held to the same two-line crop the definite-height run paints, so the record and the probe agree. The shipped `Text`'s `truncate` prop does paint the ellipsis, one line with a marked tail. A cell with **no** `overflow` lets a nowrap run paint **103** points past its 200-point edge while `bounds()` still reports the cell's width, so an overflow check reads the frame, not the layout report | one-line label = a definite width, `position: "relative"` and `overflow: "hidden"` on the cell plus `whiteSpace: "nowrap"` on the run, then `textOverflow`; for a multi-line clamp use the shipped `Text clamp` — it owns a height of `lines × lineHeight` with `overflow: "hidden"` — and do the same in a hand-written run, because the runtime runs **no** `lineClamp`. `ellipsis-start` is available when the tail must survive |
| Per-element `transform` / `rotate` / `scale` / `translate` / `skew` | not supported | no such field in `StyleDesc` | animate the numeric `MotionStyle` fields with `@gpuixui/motion`; static rotation/scale is unrepresentable |
| `zIndex` / stacking order | not supported in the published API | no `zIndex` in the published `StyleDesc`; paint order is tree order, so an overlay must render last (`toast.mdx`, `tabs.mdx`) | render the surface last in the tree. **The repo's source-built `darwin-x64` binding carries a local `zIndex` patch** (`apps/playground/patches/gpuix-zindex.patch`) — do not write against it in library code |
| Sticky positioning | not supported | **measured** by `data-grid-fixed-header`: a `position: "sticky"` child inside its scroller moved by exactly the scroll amount, i.e. it paints as `relative`. `position` is typed `string`, so the type does not say this | put the header **outside** the row scroller as a sibling; wrap header and rows in one `overflowX` scroller so both move together |
| Frozen column | supported, with limits | **Composed, not provided** — with no sticky, a frozen first column is the header rule on the other axis, and **measured** by `data-grid-frozen-column` (a 120px label pane beside a 240px grid over 60 × 28px rows): a wheel over the grid moves both panes to `-300` and a wheel over the frozen column moves both to `-600`, because the app mirrors `onScrollChange` into `scrollTo` on the other pane in **both** directions; the row under the reader keeps its `y` across panes; a `deltaX` wheel pans the grid 200px and leaves the frozen column still (a frozen column does not pan); a press on a frozen cell selects the row for the whole grid; and a `-10000` wheel clamps both panes at the same `-(60 × 28 − 240) = -1440`. **Measured in a window** by `frozen-column-lifecycle` (the same 120px column and 240px grid): the mirror lands in the **same frame** as the wheel — `scrollTo` paints synchronously from inside `onScrollChange`, so both panes report one offset after a single settle, where a mirror on the next render would leave the column one frame behind — the sideways pan is the grid's alone, and a press on a frozen cell selects the row for the grid | compose two scrollers, one per pane, and mirror their offsets through `onScrollChange` + `scrollTo`, guarding the echo with a **ref** — `scrollTo` paints synchronously, so the target's own change arrives inside the call and would otherwise mirror back. Share one row identity, set `overflowX` only on the pane that pans, and expect no single-scroller freeze |
| Table sort and selection | supported, with limits | **Composed, not provided** — a table is flex rows of fixed-width cells, and the two interactions MISSION names were **measured** by `table-lifecycle` in a window (60 rows of 28px in a 300×200 viewport, three columns in a 380px content box, so 80px of horizontal travel): a press selects the record it hit **and that row holds the focus** (`selected=r3`, focus on the pressed row), so the key that follows reaches that row's own `onKeyDown` with no synthetic focus; `down` roves focus and selection together to the next record (`selected=r4`) through the handler's own `renderer.focusElement`; a press on the size header sorts by the column it hit and a second press flips the direction (ascending, then descending) while the selected **record** survives the sort (`selected=r4` still); the header keeps its painted `y` while a −100 wheel moves the rows under it, and a wheel **over** the header scrolls nothing, because the header is a sibling outside the row scroller — moved inside it, the same wheel paints the header at `y = -36`; and one `overflowX` box around header and body pans both to the same `x` (−60), so a header cell and its column stay aligned after a pan. One measured constraint: a sortable header whose painted centre is clipped by the horizontal viewport sorts nothing, because the automation protocol aims a press at the element's centre — the probe's first run put the third column at 280…380 in a 300px frame and the press changed no order | key sort and selection by **record**, never by row index, so a sort cannot move the selection; give every row `tabIndex={0}` so the press focuses it, and move the focus with `focusElement` from the row's own key handler; keep the header a **sibling above** the row scroller and wrap both in one `overflowX` scroller; keep a sortable column's painted centre inside the frame, or pan the header before expecting a press to land |
| Composite-row press routing | supported, with limits | **Composed, not provided** — a table puts a checkbox cell, a sort arrow, an expander or a trailing menu inside the row that also selects it, and GPUI routes the press to every hitbox from the topmost down to the first element that **paints a background** (a `BlockMouseExceptScroll` hitbox, which `packages/native` inserts for a styled fill). The rule is about painted surfaces, not depth. **Measured** by `press-routing-lifecycle` (15 checks on 0.11.0) across the ten nestings a table or a tree composes; `PRESS_CASES` in `apps/playground/src/live/press-routing.ts` is the expectation, and this row prints its `formatHits` spelling: `text-unfilled → hits=row-click`, `text-filled → hits=none`, `div-filled → hits=none`, `control-filled → hits=cell-click`, `control-unfilled → hits=cell-click,row-click`, `inert-control → hits=cell-click,row-click`, `drag-row → hits=cell-click`, `aux-row → hits=none`, `field-cell → hits=row-key`, `field-in-row → hits=row-click`. A key is a different path: it travels the **focus path**, so a row's `onKeyDown` hears a key typed in a field inside it while the field keeps the key and the focus | a cell that paints must take the press itself, or paint nothing there, when the row is the selection — a row's `onClick` cannot be the only path. An unfilled child runs **both** its own handler and the row's, so fill the cell or guard the row before putting a pressable chip in a selectable row. A reorder handle must be the element the press lands on, because a row that captures through `onMouseDown` never hears a press its cell takes. Put a context menu on the cell that paints, because a row's `onAuxClick` does not hear a right press on a filled child. `pointerEvents: "none"` on a filled control still runs both handlers — only an unfilled drawing lets the press through. Read the key event's own `elementId` before acting on an arrow, because an editable cell's field keeps the key |
| Composite-row hover routing | supported, with limits | **Composed, not provided** — a row's pointer feedback is the runtime's native `style.hover` refinement, whose hovered set comes from the **same hit test** the press takes, so a child that paints a fill can take the row's hover without taking the row's press. **Measured** by `hover-routing-lifecycle` (56 checks on 0.11.0) on four 460×56 rows whose resting fill is the `card` role and whose `hover` fill is the `accent` role, each with one child at the pointer and a readout of the row's own enter and leave counts; `HOVER_CASES` in `apps/playground/src/live/hover-routing.ts` is the expectation, and this row prints it: `text-unfilled → row-enter=1 child-enter=1 paint=yes`, `text-filled → row-enter=0 child-enter=1 paint=no`, `div-filled → row-enter=0 child-enter=1 paint=no`, `inert-div → row-enter=1 child-enter=1 paint=yes`. An unfilled `<text>` label leaves the row both its enter event and its hover fill; a `<text>` or a `<div>` that paints a fill takes both; and `pointerEvents: "none"` puts the row back in the pointer's path while the child **still hears its own listener**, because opting out removes the fill's hitbox, not the handler. Two negative controls were measured: expecting the filled-`<text>` case to paint fails with `expected true, got false`, and dropping `pointerEvents: "none"` from the inert child fails with `expected 1, got 0` | give a decorative child — a status pill, a badge, a metric — `pointerEvents: "none"` so the row keeps its hover, or paint the row's `hover` on the child when the child must answer the pointer itself. A row that waits for `onMouseEnter` hears nothing behind a fill, so read the painted boundary rather than the nesting depth |
| Modifier press and chord selection | supported, with limits | **Composed, not provided** — a click carries the held modifier set and the automation protocol sends one, and the selection arithmetic is the app's. **Measured** by `modifier-select-lifecycle` (22 checks on 0.11.0): a `clickWith` press is hit-tested like a plain one, so a plain press on row 3 reports `selected=3 anchor=3` with `flags=cmd:false,ctrl:false,alt:false,shift:false` and the pressed row holding the focus; `shift` row 6 reports `selected=3,4,5,6 anchor=3` — the span, not `{3,6}` — and `shift` row 1 re-ranges backwards from the same anchor (`selected=1,2,3`); `cmd` row 5 reports `flags=cmd:true,ctrl:false,alt:false,shift:false` and `selected=1,2,3,5 anchor=5`, and pressing it again removes it (`selected=1,2,3 anchor=5`); `cmd-shift` row 2 joins the span to the set (`selected=2,3,4,5,6,7`); a plain press replaces the set and moves the anchor (`selected=4 anchor=4`), so the next `shift` ranges from that press rather than the last toggle (`selected=4,5,6,7 anchor=4`); a chord press focuses its row as a plain one does; and the chosen rows paint the theme's `selected` fill while row 0 paints none of it. One measured limit: **a double press cannot be synthesized** — `simulateClick` and the locator both dispatch `click_count: 1`, so two presses on the same row report `click=1` even in a row that listens for a double click | read the payload's four flags rather than deriving the chord from `shift` alone, because a `cmd` the runtime sent is invisible to a handler that only tests `shift`; keep the anchor as state — a **plain** press moves it, `shift` **keeps** it and a `cmd` toggle **moves** it — or a second range starts from the wrong row; give a control that opens on a double press a keyboard path or an explicit control, since no window probe can send a count |
| Filtering controls | supported | **Composed, not provided** — a filter is a `div` + `<input>` above the rows, so the runtime cannot refuse the control; the rules around it are the app's, and **measured** by `table-filter-lifecycle` (a literal, case-insensitive query above 120 rows of 28px in a 240px scroller): `1` narrows the painted rows to **39** while the field keeps the text session and the visible selection item-1 survives; `12` narrows to 2 and **clears** the selection it hid; `12z` paints the empty state and no row; clear restores 120 and parks the session back in the filter field; a −200 wheel then a filter opens the new set at offset **0** — read from `scrollOffset`, because the readout alone would have passed while the list stayed put; `Down` from `Item 1` lands on `Item 10`, the filtered order rather than the numeric one, and moves the real focus with the selection; and a clear keeps item-10, because the full set still holds it | keep the selection by **record** and clear it when the record leaves the set; leave the text session in the filter field while rows come and go; reset the scroller to the top on every filter change; walk the filtered array on arrow keys and move the focus with the selection |
| `backdropFilter` / per-element blur | not supported | no such field; blur exists only as the window backdrop | use an opaque or translucent fill |
| Window backdrop vibrancy (`windowBackground: "blurred"`) | supported, with limits | `WindowOptions.windowBackground` is honoured, not ignored. **Measured** by `window-backdrop` (one 900×520 window per run, captured at 1800×1040 — the display's 2× scale, read back rather than assumed): an `opaque` window's unpainted surface carries alpha **255**, `transparent` and `blurred` both carry alpha **0**, and the app's painted box stayed `rgba(217, 67, 43, 255)` in all three. Two limits: the app-side capture of `blurred` is **byte-identical** to `transparent`, because the vibrancy is composited behind the window, so no app frame proves the blur; and a second `init` in one process fails with `A GPUI application already exists on this thread`, so each mode is its own CLI run (`--window-background <mode>`). A translucent app fill composites against the window's surface rather than the theme literal: `#D9432B80` painted `rgba(109, 34, 22, 255)` in an opaque window | let the **unpainted** area carry the translucency and paint the content opaque; use `"blurred"` for the effect and confirm the look by eye on the target OS — do not claim it from a capture. One window per process, so a translucent and an opaque window cannot be compared in one run |
| CSS `filter`, `clip-path`, blend modes, text shadow | not supported | absent from `StyleDesc` | compose the effect from `div`/`svg` layers |
| CSS shorthand and `calc()` strings | not supported | values are numbers or colours, not a CSS string | `@gpuixui/tw` resolves a numeric `calc()` to a number before it reaches the runtime |
| Keyframe animations, variants, shared-layout transitions | not supported | `MotionProps` drives `width`, `height`, `opacity`, `top`, `right`, `bottom`, `left`, `borderRadius` with duration/delay/ease | `@gpuixui/motion` springs over those fields; a cross-fade is `opacity` |
| Animation interruption | supported | a target change starts the next transition from the current visible value | — |
| Animated geometry and content clipping | supported, with limits | `motion` tweens painted geometry. **Measured** by `motion-geometry` on a 120×40 box with `transition={{ duration: 3, ease: "easeInOut" }}`: eight presses ~50ms apart painted `40, 43, 117, 123, 221, 240, 240, 240` — a tween, not a jump — and settled at 240×240, the reverse run tweened `240, 240, 167, 63, 59.5, 40, 40, 40`, and `onMotionComplete` fired once per run. **The animated box clips its content rather than reflowing it**: a fixed 320×200 child kept that size while the box was 120×40 and while it was 240×240. `duration: 0` is the negative control (two target sizes, no frame between them) | animate an **outer** clipping box (`overflow: "hidden"`) for a collapsing sidebar or a growing row — the content inside stands still, it is not re-laid out; read `onMotionComplete` when a finished run should unmount or unlock; a numeric target lands exactly, so the settled box is the value written |
| Reduced-motion detection | not supported | **A measured absence, not an unrun probe.** `@gpuix/native` 0.11.0's `index.d.ts` publishes no preference query — no `appearance`, `prefers*` or `reduce*` export, and `WindowOptions` carries no such option — so there is no value to read. `@gpuixui/motion` already takes the policy from the caller: `MotionValue.sample(elapsedMs, reducedMotion)` jumps to the target when the boolean is `true`, and the React hooks take `options.reducedMotion`. No library component reads a system preference | the app owns the policy: detect it in the app's own layer, pass the boolean into `@gpuixui/motion` (see [Motion](/docs/motion)), and keep motion optional and short — never the carrier of meaning |
| Custom paint | not supported | `canvas` is in `ElementType` but blank | render `svg`; there is no retained draw API |
| Inline SVG painting | supported, with limits | `<svg source>` paints its markup, but **as one tint**: `@gpuix/native`'s `SvgElement` renders through GPUI's icon element, so the whole markup is masked in `style.color` and a shape's own `fill` never reaches the frame. **Measured** by `content-surfaces`: all five shapes painted in the one source tint, while five stacked `<svg>` elements each painted their own (bar green, bubble blue, line red, axis yellow, label orange); `<text>` inside the markup paints. **Where the path lands** is measured by `svg-pattern` (34 checks): a generated `<path>` is scaled from its view box by the element's **width alone** — a 60-unit square stroked 10 in a 100-unit view box paints 140×140 in a 200×200 element (its stroke included, centred), the same view box paints 300×30 in a 300×100 element and 100×10 in a 100×200 element, so the height is never consulted and a stroke's weight follows the width — the view box **clips** its own overflow (a band drawn from −40 to 140 paints 200×40 and nothing outside), one `<path>` of 20 generated subpaths paints **20/20**, and a resize to 300×100 re-lays the path out at 210 wide while the square content area (300 tall) paints **above and below** the 100-point element, because the element does not clip it | stack one `<svg>` per colour — a multi-series chart is one element per series; labels can live in the markup, but a gradient or a per-shape palette inside one element is not available. Size a pattern or line-art field **near-square**: the element scales by width and centres a content area it does not clip, so a 3:1 box paints its line art above and below the box and over whatever is painted after it |
| Container queries | not supported | no such field; `useWindowSize` is the responsive hook | measure the box (`onLayout`, on `div`/`text`) or `useWindowSize` and branch in the component |
| Canvas drawing | not supported | as above — no 2D context, and **measured** by `content-surfaces`: a `<canvas>` tracks no painted bounds and paints nothing, so the fill behind it shows through | as above |
| IME composition | not supported | no composition events reach React | compose from `onInput`/`onKeyDown` at the application layer; live IME preview needs a runtime change |
| Native text host ink | supported, with limits | Two inks inside a field are the runtime's rather than a `StyleDesc`'s, and **measured** by `field-ink` (22 checks on 0.11.0). ## 4. Accessibility and motion, cross-cutting

The gap components cite most often is the aria subset above: a checkbox cannot
announce `checked`, a slider cannot announce a numeric value, and a control
cannot be named by a sibling `Label`. Each component documents the consequence
on its own page rather than implying the attribute exists.

A window's `Tab` traversal focuses the element **without running React's
`onFocus`** on 0.11.0 — measured by `tabs-lifecycle` (the trigger's counter reads
**`focuses=0`** for the traversal, three clean runs on 2026-10-22) and
`tooltip-lifecycle` (the trigger is focused and the tip stays closed). The
offscreen `TestRenderer` does dispatch the event — its `focus-traversal`
scenario reads `traversal dispatched onFocus` — so a headless suite cannot stand
in for a window here. A focus cue therefore belongs at **rest** (every shipped
ring is), a focus-only surface needs a press path, and a key *move* still reads
the key event's own `elementId` or `getFocusedElementId`: the first arrow after a
traversal must move off the tab it reached rather than the selection, which the
event names directly.

Assistive technology sees a node only when it has **both** an id (always set) and
a `role`; `"none"`/`"presentation"` remove the node. The `a11y-tree` scenario
read the tree those rules produce: the role reaches the platform under its own
name (`option` is `ListBoxOption`), the seven `aria-*` attributes land on
`label`/`description`/`author_id`/`selected`/`expanded`/`level`/`value`, a
`<text>` is a `Label`, and a roleless `div` carrying only an `aria-label` is
absent from the tree. That tree is the offscreen `TestRenderer`'s; a window's
automation protocol has no a11y verb, so it is not among the window probes above.
Focus rings are painted by `focusVisible`, which is measured to paint after Tab
and never after a mouse press — the tokens package owns the helper
(`keyboardFocusRing`) and the policy.

Motion is springs over numeric fields only, with no keyframes and no preference
query: the runtime exposes no reduced-motion flag, so `@gpuixui/motion` takes it
as a caller-supplied boolean (`MotionValue.sample(elapsedMs, reducedMotion)` and
the hooks' `options.reducedMotion`), and the app owns the policy. The
state-driven design rule is to make motion optional and short, never the carrier
of meaning. Where it runs it is a real tween on painted
geometry: a 3s box painted eight intermediate heights between 40 and 240, landed
exactly at 240, and fired `onMotionComplete` once (`motion-geometry`). The
animated box **clips** its content — a fixed 320×200 child kept its size while
the box was 120×40 and while it was 240×240 — so a collapsing sidebar animates an
outer `overflow: "hidden"` box instead of expecting the panel inside to re-lay
out.
